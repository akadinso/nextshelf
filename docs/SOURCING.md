## Compliance: read this before importing electrical items

- The NRCS requires a **Letter of Authority (LOA)** for electrical and electronic apparatus connected to mains electricity before it can clear customs or be sold. Portable fans are covered (you can find LOAs for portable fans under IEC 60335-2-80). The NRCS has said electrical products are the most common non-compliant items found online, and there have been raids.
- Everything seeded here is gravity, hand, battery or USB powered, so the risk is lower than for plug-in appliances. **I could not confirm whether USB-only devices, or the USB chargers that ship with them, need an LOA.** Ask the NRCS directly before you import the USB items (#11, #14 to #17).
- Avoid mains-powered products (plug-in fans, hot and cold dispensers, UV sterilisers, RO pumps) unless the importer or supplier holds a valid NRCS LOA that covers your exact model.
- Chinese plugs do not fit SA sockets. If you ever sell plug-in items, they need an SA-standard plug.

## Honest descriptions (Consumer Protection Act)

- Say what the product does, not more. "Reduces chlorine, sediment and odours" is fine if the cartridge type supports it. "Kills 99.9% of bacteria" needs a test report.
- Do not call a water filter a substitute for treating unsafe water, and be careful with borehole or well water. Say what the supplier's documents support.
- Set delivery expectations clearly. CJ shipping to SA can take weeks, and you should check whether customers could be charged import VAT or duty on arrival.

## Pricing check

For each product: **landed cost** (CJ price + shipping to SA) + **PayFast fee** + your **margin** (+ VAT if you are VAT-registered) = minimum selling price. Compare with SA shops before you finalise.
Some market reference points seen while researching: rechargeable USB clip-on fans around R213 locally; AC/DC rechargeable fans with a light roughly R1,400 to R1,700; stainless gravity filters roughly R1,400 to R4,500; a Tata Swach 15L gravity purifier around R1,500.
